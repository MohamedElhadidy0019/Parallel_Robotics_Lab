What each block is actually doing

Setup, once: before the loop ever starts, candidate camera poses around the object are IK-filtered for reachability by CuRobo and cached to disk, and the known CAD mesh is sampled into a fixed 5,000-point checklist — this is what coverage gets measured against.
  1. 1
    Score candidates. For every reachable, not-yet-tried pose, GPU ray-casting (our own implementation) counts how many currently-unseen checklist points it would reveal — front-facing and not blocked by the object's own body. nbv_core/ray_scoring.py
  2. 2
    Pick best view. Greedy argmax over those scores — whichever candidate promises the most new coverage, no multi-step lookahead. nbv_planner.py
  3. 3
    Move arm. CuRobo's MotionGen plans and executes a collision-aware trajectory to that pose — self-collision and object/table collision, not hand-written. nbv_core/motion_planning.py
  4. 4
    Capture + update. A real depth image is taken, filtered to the object, back-projected to 3D, and matched against the checklist (nearest-neighbor, 6mm tolerance) to mark points confirmed seen. nbv_core/coverage.py
Loop: steps 1–4 repeat against the same cached candidates and the same known mesh, using the growing seen/unseen state each time. Stop: once confirmed coverage reaches 92.5%, or the best remaining candidate no longer offers a worthwhile view.
